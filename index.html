How to play

One player is the Thrower 🧱, the other is the Hunter 🎯. The tower in the middle is a stack of seven stones.

1 · Knock it down

  • The Thrower stands behind the white line. Drag backwards and release (like a slingshot) to roll the ball at the tower.
  • You get 3 throws. Miss all three and the roles swap.

2 · Rebuild vs. hunt

  • When the tower falls, the stones scatter and both players can run.
  • Thrower: pick up the stones biggest first (the glowing one is next) and carry each back to the tower spot. Stack all seven and you score a point — “Haft Sang!”. You can also run into the ball to kick it away from the Hunter.
  • Hunter: run to the ball to pick it up, then tap/click where you want to throw. Hit the Thrower and the roles swap. While holding the ball you can't move, and you must throw within 5 seconds — hold it longer and the Thrower gets a stone placed for free. You're not allowed inside the red defense arc around the tower.

Controls

  • Computer: move with WASD or the arrow keys; use the mouse to aim and throw.
  • Phone: drag in the bottom-left corner of the field as a joystick; tap or drag anywhere else to aim and throw.

First player to 3 points wins the match.

About the game · دربارهٔ هفت سنگ

Haft Sang (“seven stones”) is a traditional Iranian team game played in villages and city streets across the country, by kids and adults alike. Old books and sources suggest it has been a favorite pastime in Iran for centuries, though no one can pin down exactly when it began. It needs almost no equipment — seven flat stones and a ball — which is a big part of why it spread so widely.

Why seven? The number seven carries special meaning in Persian culture and far beyond it — from the Seven Labours of Rostam in the Shahnameh to the seven items of the Nowruz haft-sin table.

How it's traditionally played: two teams face off. The attacking team throws the ball from behind a line (often about seven steps away) to knock down the stack. Then they scatter and try to rebuild it, while the defending team throws the ball at them — anyone who gets hit is out. Stack all seven before everyone is out, and your team scores.

Official rules: for most of its history the game had no single rulebook. Iran's Federation of Traditional and Indigenous Sports later wrote standard rules, including a defense arc that defenders may not cross and a 5-second rule for holding the ball. This online version borrows both.

With less space and fewer neighborhood gatherings in city life, Haft Sang is played far less than it used to be. Bringing it online is a small way to keep it alive — and to share it with people who've never heard of it.

Source: “The history of Haft Sang” (in Persian), Espadaan Magazine. This online version is simplified to two players, one on each side.
